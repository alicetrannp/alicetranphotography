What is Tea Ceremony?

A tea ceremony is a rich cultural tradition celebrated across various Asian cultures, each with its own unique style and significance. In China, the tea ceremony, known as "Gongfu Cha," emphasizes the artful preparation and enjoyment of tea, showcasing precision and respect in every step of the process.

Incorporating a tea ceremony into a wedding is a cherished tradition that symbolizes respect, gratitude, and the joining of families. During this ritual, the newlyweds serve tea to their parents and elders as a gesture of appreciation and to receive blessings for their future together. It's a moment of profound significance, where cultural heritage meets familial bonds, emphasizing respect for tradition and honoring the wisdom and guidance of older generations. This ritual not only enriches the wedding celebration but also serves as a beautiful expression of unity and continuity within Chinese culture.

In Vietnam, the tea ceremony is part of the engagement ceremony, known as "Lễ Ăn Hỏi" or "Đám Hỏi." The groom's family arrives at the bride's home carrying gift trays called "mâm quả," and the two families formally meet and welcome each other. The couple lights incense at the family altar to honor their ancestors, then serves tea to their parents and elders, who share their blessings and gifts for the marriage. It is a heartfelt celebration of family and heritage on both the bride's and the groom's side.

In Korea, "Paebaek" is a traditional wedding ceremony where tea is served to honor and seek blessings from the elders, symbolizing the couple's respect and commitment.

In the Philippines, the "money dance" or "money dance tradition" is a customary part of weddings. During this joyful celebration, guests pin money onto the bride's gown or veil, or the groom's suit, as they dance together. This tradition symbolizes prosperity and a heartfelt gesture from guests, showing their support and best wishes for the couple's future together.

Each of these ceremonies provides a window into the values and aesthetics of its culture, offering a moment of connection and reflection through the simple yet profound act of sharing tea with your loved ones.
